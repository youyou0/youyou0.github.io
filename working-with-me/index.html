Working with me

I work on a problem until I understand it: how the thing really behaves, before any fix. I'd rather ship the smallest thing that works than a big one I can't explain.

How I decide

  • A rule of the real world before a tuned threshold.

    When two lanes overlapped, I did not tune a cut-off: real lanes run side by side, so the lane whose shape breaks gives way. Distances are measured in car widths, not in a share of the image, so perspective is accounted for.

    See it: The shape before the heat, what it was →

  • A guard on the result, without waiting.

    When one lane spilled over the other, I put a guard on the output straight away: the test cases that failed before it pass after.

    See it: The shape before the heat, a first answer →

How I run a piece of work

  • I write down what was seen before looking for a fix.

    Then I find out how the thing really behaves, decide how the result will be proven, make the smallest change that can meet it, and replay the same proof after. The verdict comes last.

    See it: The shape before the heat, from what was odd to the proof →

  • I say what I saw, what I suppose and what I guess.

    Each cause is given with its certainty: observed, supported, or a guess. A fix waits for an observed cause, or one confirmed by an experiment.

    See it: The shape before the heat, what I expected and what I saw →

  • One use case first, then the guarantee that protects the rest.

    To stop a zone move from resetting a whole camera, a first plan had fourteen use cases and forty-three tests. I kept the one that answered the need, only the zone moved and nothing reloads, then asked for its opposite: a real change must still reach everyone.

    See it: Traffic statistics, a zone move no longer resets the camera →

  • A clean result is weighed before it counts as a pass.

    Testing a colleague’s fix for a rare clash between two cameras: a week without a single error. I counted how often the clash could have happened at all: once, for 98 seconds. The fix passed, and the report says exactly what the week proved.

    See it: Journal, week 45 →

  • I write down the leads that died, and what killed them.

    Comparing slopes, one line through a whole lane, a threshold in pixels: each was tried, each failed for a reason worth keeping, so no one walks it twice.

    See it: The shape before the heat, the search →

With an AI agent

  • I set the rules and give the verdict; the agent measures, tests and writes.

    On the overlapping lanes, I set the domain rule; the agent proposed the measures and thresholds, which I checked; a test agent wrote the tests before any change, and they failed for the right reason first.

    See it: Lanes that draw themselves, my part →

  • The agent traces the path; I choose where the decision lives.

    The agent followed a configuration change through five services and read the installation logs. I chose that each detector decides for itself, rather than the server, and set up and read the test on a full installation.

    See it: Traffic statistics, a zone move no longer resets the camera →

  • One frame for every piece of work.

    The same six steps for every task, from what was seen to a verdict, each change proved before and after; the cockpit shows where each piece of work stands and who it waits for.

    See it: Working with AI agents, the whole case →

With a team

  • I review my colleagues’ work on real data.

    I review and approve colleagues’ pull requests on the modules I know, and test their fixes as the tester when asked: two 7-day runs on live servers, each judged against how often the bug could have fired.

    See it: Journal, week 45 →

  • I explain the work as it goes.

    Each subject has its decks, written along the way for the team: 12 on the counting zone that places itself, 26 on the lanes learned from traffic.

    See it: Lanes that draw themselves, the path →

  • I build the tools that make a result visible.

    A viewer that shows what a unit test really builds, a tool that sets automatic counts against hand counts, a viewer of how a zone settles lane by lane.
