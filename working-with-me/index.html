Working with me

I work on a problem until I understand it: how the thing really behaves, before any fix. I'd rather ship the smallest thing that works than a big one I can't explain. And sometimes the honest answer is no.

How I decide

  • A rule of the real world before a tuned threshold.

    When two lanes overlapped, I did not tune a cut-off: real lanes run side by side, so the lane whose shape breaks gives way. Distances are measured in car widths, not in a share of the image, so perspective is accounted for.

    See it: The shape before the heat, what it was →

  • A guard on the result when the cause is still unknown.

    Why a lane spilled over the other was never found. I put a guard on the output rather than wait: seven of eight test cases failed before it, seven of eight passed after.

    See it: The shape before the heat, a first answer →

  • The verdict can be no.

    The counting zone that places itself nearly matched a zone placed by hand, but lost about two points where counting was good. I shipped it switched off, behind a setting, until it is measured on more sequences.

    See it: Traffic statistics, the key decision →

How I run a piece of work

  • I write down what was seen before looking for a fix.

    Then I find out how the thing really behaves, decide how the result will be proven, make the smallest change that can meet it, and replay the same proof after. The verdict comes last, and it can be no.

    See it: The shape before the heat, from what was odd to the proof →

  • I say what I saw, what I suppose and what I guess.

    Each cause is given with its certainty: observed, supported, or a guess. A fix waits for an observed cause, or one confirmed by an experiment.

    See it: The shape before the heat: two causes left open →

  • I write down the leads that died, and what killed them.

    Comparing slopes, one line through a whole lane, a threshold in pixels: each was tried, each failed for a reason worth keeping, so no one walks it twice.

    See it: The shape before the heat, the search →

With an AI agent

  • I set the rules and give the verdict; the agent measures, tests and writes.

    On the overlapping lanes, I set the domain rule; the agent proposed the measures and thresholds, which I checked; a test agent wrote the tests before any change, and they failed for the right reason first.

    See it: Lanes that draw themselves, my part →

With a team

  • I review my colleagues’ work on real data.

    I review and approve colleagues’ pull requests on the modules I know, and test their fixes as the tester when asked: a 7-day run on live cameras for one of them.

  • I explain the work as it goes.

    Each subject has its decks, written along the way for the team: 12 on the counting zone that places itself, 26 on the lanes learned from traffic.

    See it: Lanes that draw themselves, the path →

  • I build the tools that make a result visible.

    A viewer that shows what a unit test really builds, a tool that sets automatic counts against hand counts, a viewer of how a zone settles lane by lane.
