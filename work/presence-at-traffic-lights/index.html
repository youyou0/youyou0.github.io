Presence at traffic lights

At a junction, the traffic light needs to know who is waiting: a car at the stop line, a cyclist, a pedestrian at the crossing. I designed and wrote the algorithm that tells it, from a camera. The embedded team then ported it into the camera itself.

The idea, drawn

Problem

A camera can replace the loops in the road, if it never hesitates.

It has to say, reliably and fast, who is in each zone: never a pedestrian taken for a car, a car counted in the next lane, or a waiting car forgotten because it stopped moving.

Goal

Presence as reliable as a loop in the road.

More than 95 % of occupancy detected, fewer than 3 % of calls missed, in under 150 ms.

My part

I created the module and wrote its algorithm.

With its unit tests and the tools to judge it against ground truth, over eight months in 2025. The embedded team ported it to run inside the camera; the counting, the web interface and the link to the traffic controller are their work.

The path

  1. Jan 2025

    Who is in the zone?

    Tracked objects of every kind cross the junction: cars, bikes, scooters, pedestrians.

    Decided: sort the tracks by kind of road user, and let an object enter a zone only in the direction of its lane.

  2. Spring 2025

    Five kinds of zone

    A stop line, a crossing and a waiting area do not wait for the same road users, nor in the same way.

    Decided: five zone types (stop line for vehicles, stop line for bikes, crossing, waiting area, advance zone), each with its own road users and its own rules.

  3. Apr – Jun 2025

    When is a car really there?

    A car's track is a single point; it says little about how much of the car is in the zone, or in which lane.

    Decided: a car is present when a quarter of its box is in the zone; its lane is the one whose axis is closest; presence is held five frames after the car is lost.

  4. May – Aug 2025

    Still things, and every site different

    A shape that never moves can look like a car, and a waiting pedestrian stops moving too.

    Decided: drop boxes that match the empty background, keep a waiting area on once someone has stood in it, and give every lane its own sensitivity.

  5. Jun 2025 – Oct 2026

    Into the camera

    The product runs inside the camera, not on a server.

    Decided: the embedded team ported my latest version to the camera; in October 2026 I reviewed the removal of the server version it replaced.

Where it stands

  • 97.9 %Occupancy detected, against a target above 95 %.
  • 2.1 %Calls missed, against a target below 3 %.
  • 133 msAverage delay, against a target below 150 ms.

Key decision

The box decides, not the track

Context
A presence at the stop line was triggered by the vehicle's track, a single point on the road.
Decision
Use the vehicle's whole box: present when at least a quarter of it is inside the zone, in the lane whose axis is closest.
Set aside
The track point alone.
