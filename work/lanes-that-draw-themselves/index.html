Lanes that draw themselves

A road camera that can turn has to know where the lanes are every time it stops on a new view, with no one to draw them. I wrote the module that learns them by watching the traffic: where the cars drive is the road.

The idea, drawn

Problem

A camera that turns loses its lanes.

The software that detects incidents, such as a car driving the wrong way or stopped on the shoulder, has to know where the lanes are. When the camera turns, the lanes drawn by hand no longer fit.

Goal

Lanes as a person would draw them, found alone.

Within minutes of the camera stopping, on any road.

My part

I created the module and wrote its algorithm.

From the cars' paths to the published lanes. A colleague connected it to the camera geometry and to the signal that the camera has moved. Since 2026 I work with an AI agent: I frame each problem and set the rules; the agent writes the tests first, then the change.

The path

  1. Oct 2024 – Jan 2025

    Where do the cars drive?

    The camera only sees vehicles moving; the road itself has to be inferred.

    Decided: paint every car's move between two frames into a map of where cars drive, one map per direction, and keep its hottest part.

  2. 2025

    Not every lane is as busy

    A quiet lane is much fainter on the map than a busy one, so some lanes were missed.

    Decided: lower the bar step by step until the lane's edges are supported, then cut the lane into 15 bands, each with straight edges.

  3. Sep 2025 – Jan 2026

    Bad paths, bad lanes

    Broken or zig-zagging paths drew false lanes; one case produced thousands of lanes and brought a video service down.

    Decided: keep only clean car paths, check each lane's size and coverage, and publish one lane per direction. First release in December 2025.

  4. Oct 2026

    The hard shoulder

    Nobody drives on the shoulder, so it was never part of a lane, yet a broken-down car stops exactly there.

    Decided: widen each lane on its outer side by one and a half car widths, measured on the cars at that distance.

  5. Oct 2026

    Two directions on top of each other

    Each direction is learned on its own, so their lanes could overlap, and an incident there would belong to both.

    Decided: the lane that bends out of its own line gives way; traffic decides only when the shape can't tell.

Where it stands

  • 4–8 minTo build a lane after the camera stops, once 100 cars are seen.
  • Oct 2026The hard shoulder included, shipped.
  • 4 / 4Overlapping lane pairs resolved on a 24-hour real sequence (4 of 20 pairs overlapped). Approved, waiting to be merged.

Key decision

The shape decides before the traffic

Context
Two lanes claim the same space; the rule gave it to the busier one, which in one case cut the correct, straight lane.
Decision
Each lane is compared with its own course; the one that bends clearly more loses the space.
Set aside
Traffic alone; comparing slopes, since a swing is as straight as the rest, only shifted.
Cost
A lane that overlaps along its whole length has no course to compare with, so traffic still decides there.
